Introduction

Breast cancer is a malignant tumor arising from breast epithelial cells, most commonly from ducts or lobules. It is characterized by uncontrolled cell growth and the potential to invade or metastasize.

Types

IDC
ILC
DCIS
TNBC
HER2+
IBC

Risk Factors

Age, female sex, BRCA mutations, family history, obesity, alcohol consumption, hormone therapy, and radiation exposure increase risk.

Symptoms

Breast lump, nipple discharge, skin dimpling, breast asymmetry, redness, and lymph node swelling.

Diagnosis

Mammography, ultrasound, MRI, and biopsy are used for diagnosis. Biopsy remains the gold standard.

Treatment

Surgery, chemotherapy, radiotherapy, hormone therapy, targeted therapy, and immunotherapy are used depending on stage and subtype.

Introduction

Breast cancer is a malignant tumor that starts in breast cells and can spread to other parts of the body. It is the most common cancer in women worldwide.

Types

IDC, ILC, DCIS, HER2-positive, Triple-negative, Inflammatory breast cancer.

Risk Factors

Age, genetics (BRCA1/2), obesity, alcohol, hormones, radiation exposure.
